- question: Is a steamer actually as effective as an iron?
  answer: For most everyday wrinkles in shirts, dresses, and curtains, yes — steam relaxes the fabric fibres without needing a hard flat surface. For crisp, sharp creases (formal trousers, dress shirt collars and cuffs), an iron still does a cleaner job, so many households keep both rather than replacing one with the other.
- question: What fabrics should I avoid steaming?
  answer: Waxed or oiled outerwear, suede, and some leather trims can be damaged by direct steam — check the garment's care label first. Delicate fabrics like silk are usually fine but benefit from holding the steamer a little further away.
- question: Handheld or upright — which is better?
  answer: A handheld steamer is faster to get going, cheaper, and stores in a drawer, making it the better fit for most flats and everyday touch-ups. An upright model with a garment hanger is easier on your arm for full outfits, curtains, or steaming several items in one session, but takes up more storage space.

A steamer that takes five minutes to heat up or runs out of water halfway through a shirt isn't saving you any time over an iron. Here's what actually works, from quick handheld models to full upright setups.

## What actually matters

- **Genuine heat-up time**, not just the marketed figure — 25-30 seconds is realistic for a decent handheld; anything claiming near-instant steam from cold is worth treating with scepticism.
- **Continuous steam output**, since a unit that sputters or needs constant refilling mid-garment defeats the point.
- **Tank size versus how you'll use it** — a small detachable tank is fine for a quick touch-up, but a full outfit or curtains benefit from a larger tank so you're not refilling repeatedly.
- **Fabric safety features**, such as an anti-drip design and a nozzle that won't scorch delicate fabrics if held too close.
