- question: Do vacuum sealers actually extend food's shelf life that much?
  answer: Yes, meaningfully — removing air significantly slows oxidation and freezer burn, commonly extending freezer storage from a few months to closer to a year for many foods, though exact times still depend on the food itself and freezer temperature.
- question: Can I use any bag, or do I need specific ones?
  answer: Most machines need textured/embossed vacuum bags or rolls specifically designed to let air channel out during sealing — a smooth standard freezer bag generally will not seal properly, so check what bag type your chosen machine requires before buying.

A vacuum sealer that jams mid-bag or fails to pull a full seal wastes both bags and food. Here's what actually matters, and which machines hold up to regular use.

## What actually matters

- **A reliable, consistent seal** — check reviews specifically for jamming or incomplete-seal complaints, which are the most common failure point.
- **Compatible bag type and cost** — some machines lock you into proprietary rolls that get expensive; check ongoing bag costs before buying, not just the machine price.
- **A moist/dry food setting** — genuinely useful for sealing anything with liquid content like marinated meat, without it being sucked into the machine.
- **Storage footprint** — full-size machines take up real counter or cupboard space; factor that in alongside price.
