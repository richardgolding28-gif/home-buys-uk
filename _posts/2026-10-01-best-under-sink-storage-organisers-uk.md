- question: How do I fit an organiser around the pipes under my sink?
  answer: Measure the gap on either side of the waste trap and water pipes before buying, not just the total cupboard width. L-shaped or two-tier designs with an offset top shelf are built to straddle a central pipe run, while a single flat tray usually only works if your pipes sit to one side.
- question: Should I get a fixed shelf, a pull-out drawer, or a freestanding cabinet?
  answer: A fixed shelf is the cheapest way to double your usable space but means kneeling and reaching to the back. A pull-out or sliding drawer costs a bit more but lets you see and reach everything without unloading the cupboard. A freestanding cabinet suits an open pedestal sink with no cupboard doors at all.
- question: Will a metal or plastic organiser cope with occasional leaks and damp?
  answer: Powder-coated or rust-resistant steel holds up well under a kitchen sink where splashes and condensation are common, but check reviews for rust reports after prolonged damp. Plastic won't rust but can discolour or warp near heat sources like a dishwasher outlet, and most ranges include a drip tray or liner specifically to catch minor leaks before they reach the cupboard base.

The space under a kitchen or bathroom sink is usually the most wasted cupboard in the house — bottles tipped over, bags of cloths jammed at the back, and a pipe running straight through the middle of it. Here's what actually makes that space usable.

## What actually matters

- **A design built around the pipes**, not just a flat shelf — L-shaped or two-tier units with an offset top level work with a central waste trap instead of forcing everything to one side.
- **Pull-out or sliding access** if you can get it — reaching to the back of a deep, dark cupboard without a slide-out tray gets old fast.
- **Rust-resistant material**, since this cupboard sees more damp and the odd drip than almost anywhere else in the house.
- **Genuinely adjustable height or width**, so the unit works around your actual pipework and cupboard dimensions instead of a generic one-size listing.
- **A liner or drip tray**, which catches small leaks and spilled cleaning products before they soak into the cupboard base.
